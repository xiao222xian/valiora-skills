# Landing Page Narrative and Conversion System

Use this reference after the Brand Spine exists. Decide what the page should
reveal, prove, and ask the visitor to do. This is a story system, not a section
template.

## 1. Internal brief versus public page

| Layer | Examples | Destination |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| Internal context | names, private anecdotes, constraints, raw research, competitor notes | working notes only |
| Publishable message | audience tension, promise, mechanism, supported feature | page copy |
| Verified proof | real UI, sourced metric, approved quote, customer case | proof surfaces |
| Evidence gap | desired claim without source, missing logo, placeholder number | internal follow-up |

Prompt detail does not become public copy merely because it was useful context.
Ask: “Does a visitor need this to understand, trust, or act?”

## 2. Visitor movement

Define a page as movement across four states:

1. **Arrival:** what the visitor believes, wants, fears, or is doing now.
2. **Reframe:** the brand's point of view that changes how the situation is seen.
3. **Experience:** product behavior and evidence that make the reframe credible.
4. **Action:** the next state the visitor can enter now.

The brand may be the narrator, but the visitor remains the person whose state
changes. Product and creator/founder stories earn space when they serve that move.

## 3. Conversion brief

Define before layout:

- primary audience and triggering situation;
- current behavior or costly before-state;
- desired after-state and brand promise;
- product category, mechanism, and meaningful difference;
- primary conversion event and immediate next state;
- strongest proof and main objection;
- acquisition context, primary device, market, and locale.

Choose one primary conversion: trial, account, product intent, download, purchase,
booking, contact, lead, waitlist, or content action. A secondary CTA exists only
for a genuinely different readiness level.

## 4. Product and distribution hierarchy

Before writing sections, label each item as `value`, `mechanism`, `proof`, or
`distribution/access`. This prevents delivery choices from masquerading as the
product story.

- App, web, CLI, Skill, browser extension, package, OS, chip, store, and download
  variants are distribution by default.
- A distribution form earns strategic prominence only when it changes the user
  job, capability, promise, or meaningful boundary.
- Multiple delivery modes do not automatically create multiple products,
  audiences, feature cards, or hero branches.
- Keep download/install/platform choices in availability, pricing, or the final
  action stage. Describe them neutrally and help visitors choose efficiently.
- Preserve product mechanism language where it matters. For example, local-only
  execution can be a privacy mechanism, while Apple Silicon and Intel buttons
  remain installation variants.

Run the **distribution-inversion test**: hide every platform, package, and
installation label. The remaining hero and headline spine must still explain
what the product is, why it matters, how it works, and why it is credible.

## 5. Headline spine

Read the H1 and section headings alone. They should form a coherent argument:

```text
Brand belief or new possibility
→ tangible product/outcome
→ mechanism or transformation
→ breadth/depth through concrete artifacts
→ proof and difference
→ invitation to act
```

Headlines are not labels. “功能”“产品”“案例”“关于我们” may work in navigation,
but they rarely carry a homepage narrative.

## 6. Content-stage library

Select the smallest useful sequence and assign unequal weight.

| Stage | Visitor question | Include when | Typical treatment |
|-------|------------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Brand-led hero | What possibility is this brand opening? | Always | thesis, orientation, proof cue, action |
| Immediate product entry | Can I act on my intent now? | A real intent-to-result path exists | focused form/workbench |
| Flagship artifact | What best embodies the promise? | One product/output can carry the story | large product stage |
| Product constellation | Is there meaningful breadth? | Multiple real offers share one thesis | journey or curated family |
| Mechanism | Why does this work differently? | Behavior needs explanation | demo, annotated process, contrast |
| Outcome gallery | What can result? | Real outputs exist | selected artifacts, not filler |
| Creator/customer story | Did it matter for someone like me? | Approved evidence exists | attributable situation and result |
| Principle/craft | Why does this brand make these choices? | It earns trust or distinction | manifesto with product evidence |
| Availability/pricing | Can I use or buy it? | Targets are current and real | platform, plan, or purchase route |
| Objection | What still stops me? | A real unanswered objection remains | concise proof/FAQ |
| Closing action | What should I do now? | Long page | same promise and destination |
| Footer | Is this a legitimate, complete brand? | Real resources exist | contact, legal, product, social |

A section does not earn its place because a benchmark has one. It earns its place
by advancing understanding, trust, desire, or action.

## 7. Stage hierarchy

Avoid equal-weight section stacking. Assign roles:

- **Protagonist:** the visitor, core product, artifact, or belief that carries the page.
- **Monuments:** one or two ideas given exceptional scale and space.
- **Supporting cast:** products, features, or cases that deepen the thesis.
- **Evidence:** proof placed near the claims it supports.
- **Bridges:** short transitions that reset pacing and move the argument forward.

Alternate density, scale, alignment, and media behavior. Do not use visual novelty
to disguise repetitive content.

## 8. Copy architecture

### First viewport

Across the complete first viewport, communicate:

- recognizable belief, promise, or outcome;
- enough category/mechanism context to orient;
- one authentic product or proof cue;
- one clear next action.

The H1 may be poetic when the supporting composition resolves meaning. Avoid
abstract mission copy that leaves product and action undiscoverable.

### Body copy

- Headline advances the story.
- Supporting copy explains mechanism or important constraint.
- Caption tells the visitor what an artifact proves or enables.
- Label gives orientation without competing with the story.
- Proof language is specific, sourced, and calm.

### CTA and state copy

Use the user's next state: “开始创建”“查看真实作品”“下载 macOS 版”“预约演示”.
Write loading, success, error, login, and unavailable states in the same voice.
Avoid vague labels such as “提交” or “探索更多” when the goal is knowable.

## 9. Proof hierarchy

Strongest to weakest:

1. direct working product experience;
2. real product artifact, screenshot, or demo;
3. attributable customer/creator case with situation and result;
4. sourced metric with definition and date;
5. specific mechanism explanation;
6. generic assertion.

Generated media improves explanation and emotion but does not upgrade evidence.

## 10. Forms and prompt boxes

- Ask only what the next transaction requires.
- Provide visible or programmatic labels.
- Preserve IME composition: Enter accepts an active candidate and does not submit.
- State format requirements before error; place feedback near the field.
- Show durable success and the next step.
- Do not send field contents to analytics.

Use an input in the hero only when a real intent reaches a real result. Otherwise
use a clear CTA; a decorative prompt box creates a broken product promise.

## 11. Market and account routing

Infer domestic, overseas, or mixed from language, domain, distribution, payment,
compliance, channels, and repository locale. Market changes host reliability,
trust signals, writing style, legal surfaces, metadata, and conversion—not only
translation.

For public/authenticated states:

- public visitors receive brand, orientation, proof, and account/trial entry;
- authenticated users may receive a direct action entry only when supported;
- CTA, navigation, server, and client state must agree;
- do not invent a logged-in studio because a benchmark has one.

## 12. Blueprint acceptance

- Brand Spine and visitor movement are explicit.
- Headings alone tell a coherent, ownable story.
- One protagonist and primary action are clear.
- Stages have distinct jobs and unequal, intentional weight.
- Every public claim has matching proof or is removed.
- Content sounds like the product and brand, not the planning conversation.
- Distribution choices help the visitor act without competing with product value.
