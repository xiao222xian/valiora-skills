# Brand System for Landing Pages

Use this reference to decide what the brand should mean, remember, sound like,
look like, and do. A Brand Spine is an upstream decision system, not public copy.

## 1. Brand is a selection system

A recognizable brand consistently decides:

- which tension is worth naming;
- which promise it is prepared to make;
- which evidence it puts on stage;
- which words, images, materials, and behaviors belong;
- which popular patterns it deliberately declines;
- what action naturally follows the story.

Brand work is weak when it ends at adjectives, mood boards, or a palette. It is
useful when two plausible page choices produce different answers because the
brand system rejects one of them.

## 2. Source hierarchy

Rank sources before synthesis:

| Priority | Source | Use |
|---------:|--------|-----|
| 1 | Shipped product and approved brand truth | Defines real behavior and commitments |
| 2 | Founder/product rationale with current evidence | Explains belief and deliberate choices |
| 3 | Real customer language, cases, support, and usage | Grounds relevance and proof |
| 4 | Existing public identity and high-performing assets | Reveals equity worth preserving |
| 5 | Competitors and references | Establishes category grammar and whitespace |
| 6 | Inference or aspiration | Internal hypothesis until approved and evidenced |

Conflicts are not silently averaged. Record which source wins and why.

## 3. Brand Spine

The Brand Spine joins commercial clarity with expressive identity:

- **Category:** the shortest familiar frame for what this is.
- **Audience tension:** the contradiction, frustration, or aspiration already
  present in the visitor's life.
- **Belief:** the brand's consequential point of view.
- **Promise:** the change the brand is willing to be measured against.
- **Mechanism:** the product behavior that earns the promise.
- **Difference:** a deliberate choice visible in the experience.
- **Personality:** how the brand behaves in language, design, service, and failure.
- **Proof:** direct experience, artifacts, people, cases, or sourced facts.
- **Boundary:** what it refuses to optimize for or imitate.
- **Action:** the next state that makes the promise tangible.

Write the thesis as a compact causal idea:

```text
Because we believe [BELIEF], we help [AUDIENCE] move from [TENSION]
to [PROMISE] through [MECHANISM], and we prove it with [PROOF].
```

This template is for internal reasoning. Rewrite or discard it for public copy.

### Product value versus distribution

Classify each offer statement before it enters the page:

- **Product value:** the change or capability the visitor wants.
- **Mechanism:** the product behavior that earns that value.
- **Proof:** the artifact or evidence that makes it credible.
- **Distribution/access:** how the visitor downloads, installs, opens, invokes,
  pays for, or reaches the product.

App, web, CLI, Skill, extension, package manager, operating system, and chip
variants are distribution by default. They become part of the brand thesis only
when they materially change the promise, user job, capability, or boundary. A
privacy-preserving local architecture may be mechanism; separate macOS download
buttons are still delivery controls.

## 4. Expression systems

### Verbal identity

Define:

- characteristic verbs and nouns;
- sentence length, rhythm, and use of contrast;
- emotional temperature and confidence;
- technical vocabulary and translation rules;
- CTA verbs and status/error language;
- prohibited clichés, inflated claims, and internal jargon.

A voice guide needs examples in context: hero, product caption, CTA, proof label,
empty state, failure, and footer—not only tone adjectives.

### Visual identity

Define:

- display/body typography roles and contrast;
- palette by semantic job, not a list of hex values;
- dominant material and surface relationship;
- composition, grid, negative space, and density rhythm;
- icon, illustration, photography, screenshot, and video treatment;
- one signature motif and its restraint rule;
- motion signature and reduced-motion equivalent.

### Content identity

Define:

- the protagonist: visitor, product, creator, craft, community, or idea;
- which artifacts appear large and which remain supporting detail;
- how products are grouped: journey, constellation, cases, editions, or systems;
- what the brand repeatedly teaches or demonstrates;
- which topics and proof types do not belong on the homepage.

### Interaction character

Define how the brand:

- invites action without ambiguity;
- responds to hover, focus, loading, success, and error;
- transitions between story and product;
- handles authentication or unavailable states;
- treats keyboard, IME, reduced motion, and assistive technology as first-class.

## 5. One signature, many expressions

A signature idea should connect meaning with a repeatable form. Examples of
structures—not styles to copy—include:

- rough material becoming a finished artifact;
- a frame that opens into the product;
- editorial annotations revealing the mechanism;
- a repeated gesture that turns intention into outcome;
- a distinctive scale change reserved for the brand's strongest claims.

Use it in three or four meaningful moments. Repetition creates memory; ubiquity
turns it into wallpaper.

## 6. Brand-led first viewport

Within the first viewport, the visitor should find:

- a recognizable idea or promise;
- enough category/mechanism context to orient themselves;
- an authentic product, artifact, or proof cue;
- one clear next action.

Not every element must sit above the fold, and the H1 need not be a literal
category sentence. The total composition must resolve poetry with orientation.

## 7. Brand memory tests

### Five-second recall

After a brief view, ask what the visitor remembers:

1. What does this brand believe or make possible?
2. What does it make or help me do?
3. What visual or verbal signature remains?

### Logo-swap test

Temporarily imagine a category competitor's Logo in the header. If the page
still feels completely plausible, the page relies on category defaults rather
than identity.

### Headline-spine test

Read only H1 and section headings. They should form a coherent point of view and
story, not a navigation list of “Features / Solutions / Testimonials / FAQ”.

### Silhouette test

Blur text and imagery. Scale, rhythm, stage hierarchy, and signature composition
should still differ from a generic template.

### Behavior test

Read CTA, loading, success, and error states together. They should sound and act
like one brand, with clarity preserved under stress.

## 8. Brand handoff acceptance

- Source and inference are distinguishable.
- Existing equity has explicit preserve/strengthen/retire decisions.
- The Brand Spine explains product, difference, promise, and evidence.
- Verbal, visual, content, and interaction systems reinforce one another.
- The signature idea is implementable across desktop, mobile, and reduced motion.
- The system can reject at least three plausible but off-brand choices.
- Product value and mechanism remain dominant when distribution labels are hidden.
